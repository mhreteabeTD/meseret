1.1.1 What is science?

In the lower grades you learnt about science in general, for example environmental science. In Grade 7 and Grade 8 you study General Science, which is about things in your daily life.

Note: the printed book has no heading "1.1.1". It jumps from "1.1" to the introduction. The heading is added here so the numbering matches 1.1.2, 1.1.3 and 1.1.4.

The word science comes from the Latin word scientia, which means "knowledge". But science is not only having knowledge. Science is a systematic (orderly) method of gaining knowledge about the physical and natural world and about human society. It is a way of learning what things are like, and it is based on observation and evidence.

Science can be indigenous or conventional.

Indigenous science is the process by which indigenous people build up their knowledge from experience of their natural environment. This knowledge rests on social, physical and spiritual understanding.

Conventional science is the system of knowledge that relies on laws established by applying the scientific method to events in the world around us. It is commonly called Western science. Indigenous science, by contrast, brings in local people's knowledge and viewpoints.

Note: modern science has grown from the work of people in many cultures, so "Western" is only a common label, not the whole story.

There are two main reasons. First, it makes us more aware of our own culture and identity. Second, it lets us bring indigenous knowledge together with Western science.

1.1.2 Branches of science

Science has two major categories: natural science and social science. Natural science is the study of nature and natural laws. It includes chemistry, biology and physics.

  • Biology is the branch of natural science that studies living things.
  • Chemistry deals with the properties, make-up, structure and changes of substances.
  • Physics studies the nature of matter and energy and how they interact.

There is no clear border line between the branches of natural science. Their knowledge overlaps. For example, chemistry and physics together are studied as physical science (physical chemistry): the study of the properties of materials and how they interact.

  • Biophysics combines biology and physics. It studies physical events and processes in living things, on scales from molecules to cells, tissues and whole organisms.
  • Biochemistry combines biology and chemistry. It explores the chemical processes inside living things, that is, the chemical reactions in them.

1.1.3 Science and technology

You have seen what science is. Now let us see what technology is.

Technology is the use of scientific knowledge to help people work more easily, live better and enjoy their environment more. It uses materials, tools, techniques and sources of power to make life easier and more pleasant, and work more productive. In short, it puts scientific knowledge into practice.

Cars, TV sets, radios, bulbs, microchips, computers, airplanes and home appliances are all products of technology.

A person who studies technology is called a technologist. Technologists use science and mathematics knowledge and skills to produce very useful tools.

Science and technology are closely connected, but they are different fields driven by different ideas and processes. Science produces knowledge for its own sake, to propose and test explanations. Technology develops human-made solutions to real-world problems. When science uses technology to gain knowledge, technology in turn uses scientific knowledge to make solutions.

1.2.2 Laboratory safety rules

A laboratory is a place of discovery and learning. But because of the nature of lab work, it can also be a place of danger if the right scientific precautions are not taken. Follow these safety rules when you do an activity in the laboratory. Tap a rule to see why it matters (the reasons are added to help).

Dress properly: goggles, gloves, shoes and lab coat.
They protect your eyes, hands, feet, skin and clothes from spills, splashes and heat.
Tie back loose hair.
Loose hair can catch fire or fall into chemicals.
Know where safety equipment, such as the fire extinguisher, is kept.
In an emergency there is no time to search for it.
Know what to do in case of an accident.
Knowing the steps lets you act quickly and tell the teacher at once.
Do not taste or smell chemicals.
Many chemicals are poisonous or harm your nose, throat and lungs.
Do not eat or drink in the laboratory.
Chemicals could get into your food or drink.
Never add water to concentrated acid. Always add acid to water. Remember the Amharic "AW! Not WA!" (A = acid, W = water).
Water poured into strong acid can heat up and splash out, burning you. Acid added slowly to water is safer.
Carry out only the experiments assigned by your teacher.
Your teacher has checked that those experiments are safe.
Dispose of all chemical wastes properly.
Waste can harm people and the environment if it is thrown away carelessly.

The use of scientific knowledge to help people work more easily and live better; putting scientific knowledge into practice.

Science produces knowledge for its own sake, to propose and test explanations. Technology uses that knowledge to make human-made solutions to real-world problems. They are linked but are different fields.

Date, title, objective, theory, materials or equipment, chemicals used, procedure, observation, result, discussion and conclusion.
